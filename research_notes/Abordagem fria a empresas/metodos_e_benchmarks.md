# B2B Cold Outreach (Abordagem fria a empresas): Methods, Frameworks and Benchmark Data (2023–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-23. All figures are vendor-reported platform data unless stated otherwise; no independent/peer-reviewed benchmark study was found. Where a page was gated or returned an error, this is noted.

## Key Question 1 — Current benchmark numbers (cold e-mail, cold call, LinkedIn) and the datasets behind them

### Takeaway
Headline cold-email "reply rates" range from 0.45% to 8.5% depending almost entirely on the denominator (per e-mail sent vs. per contact vs. per campaign) and the sender population; the most recent large platform datasets (Belkins 7.5M e-mails 2025, Smartlead 850M+ e-mails 2026, Instantly "billions" 2025) converge on well under 1% per e-mail sent for a typical sender and ~3–4% for strong senders, while cold calling connects on ~5% of dials (13% for top reps) and converts ~2–5% of conversations into meetings, and LinkedIn connection requests are accepted ~26–30% of the time with ~7–10% reply rates.

### Cited Findings

**Cold e-mail — platform datasets**
- Belkins 2025 follow-up study: 7,530,489 e-mails sent over 12 months of 2025; reply rate per e-mail step: step 1 = 0.59%, step 3 = 0.30%, step 4 = 0.34%, step 6 = 0.28%; step 1 alone yields 41.4% of all replies, steps 2–6 yield 58.6% — [Belkins, Sales follow-up statistics 2025](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics)
- Belkins 2025 study: appointment (meeting) rate ≈0.2% per e-mail from step 3 onward, roughly double steps 1–2; step 3 alone produced 35.6% of e-mail-sourced meetings (more than steps 1 and 2 combined); steps 3–5 produced 53.5% of e-mail-sourced meetings — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics)
- Belkins 2025 study, channel engagement: calls connected with 18.6% of reached prospects vs. 5.04% for LinkedIn and 0.45% for e-mail (the 0.45% figure is the "unique replies ÷ e-mails sent, excluding auto-replies/bounces" reply rate widely quoted second-hand) — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics); second-hand quote of 0.45% — [Something Inc.](https://somethinginc.com/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmark-contradiction/)
- Belkins "B2B Cold Outreach Benchmarks 2025" report (with Reply.io, Expandi, Nooks): 16.5M cold e-mails, 20M+ LinkedIn outreach attempts, 5M+ cold calls analysed; the benchmark values themselves are gated behind a download form and could not be read — [Belkins report page](https://belkins.io/resources/b2b-cold-outreach-benchmarks-2025-report). Second-hand summaries of this report give an average B2B cold e-mail reply rate of 5.8% in 2024 (down from 6.8% in 2023) and an 8.4% reply rate for single-e-mail sequences — [search summary citing Belkins](https://belkins.io/resources/b2b-cold-outreach-benchmarks-2025-report); Expandi cites the Belkins average cold-e-mail reply rate as 5.1%, "down from 7% the prior year" — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040). These 5–8% figures are evidently a different denominator (per contact/sequence) than the 0.45–0.59% per-e-mail figures above.
- Smartlead "State of Cold Email 2026": 850M+ e-mails, senders with ≥500 e-mails, coverage Jan–Jun 2026. Reply performance in "contacts per reply" (reply rate): top 5% = 26 (3.85%); top 10% = 38 (~2.63%); top 25% = 70 (1.43%); typical = 135 (0.74%); bottom 25% = 270 (~0.37%); bottom 10% = 625 (~0.16%). Top 10% is 3.5x more efficient than the typical sender. Typical bounce rate 1.54% (danger line 2%); worst quartile bounces ≥3.21% — [Smartlead benchmarks](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks)
- Smartlead Q1–Q3 2025 "Cold Email Blueprint": 3.2M+ e-mails, 1,000+ domains, 80+ countries, Jan–Sep 2025 (PDF not opened; sample size from search result) — [Smartlead PDF](https://marketing-assets.smartlead.ai/guides/Cold_Email_Blueprint_Q1_Q3_2025.pdf)
- Instantly 2026 Cold Email Benchmark Report (2025 campaign data, "billions" of sends, Jan–Dec 2025; one secondary source says Jan 1–Dec 18, 2025): platform-wide average reply rate 3.43% (replies ÷ total e-mails sent), top quartile ≈5.5%, top 10% >10.7%; ~58% of replies come from the first e-mail; sequences of 4–7 steps with e-mails under 80 words cited as optimal — [Instantly AI sales agent benchmarks page (cites report)](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sales-agent-benchmarks-2026-the-complete-performance-report/); [Instantly email sequence benchmarks 2026](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-benchmarks-2026-whats-a-good-open-rate-reply-rate-and-cost-per-meeting/); [lemlist benchmarks page citing Instantly](https://lemlist.com/blog/cold-email-benchmarks/). The primary report page itself was not opened.
- Instantly's benchmark guide (methodology framing): "effective reply rate" = positive + qualified neutral replies; reply rate formula = replies ÷ delivered e-mails ×100 excluding hard bounces; bands: 5–10% "solid", 10–15% "excellent", 15%+ "best in class on tight segments"; by motion: broad ICP on new domain = low single digits; segmented verified list with concise offer and 1–2 follow-ups = 5–10%; high-intent triggers with multi-point personalization = 10–20%+; recommends tracking meetings per 1,000 sends — [Instantly reply rate benchmarks](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)
- Hunter "State of Cold Email 2025": 11M cold e-mails sent in 2024 + survey of 217 decision-makers; average campaign reply rate 4.1% (95.9% unanswered); campaigns with ≤50 recipients = 5.8% vs. 1,000+ recipients = 2.1%; campaigns with follow-ups 4.9% vs. 3% without; Outlook with custom domain = 5.9% (highest setup); tracked (open-tracked) campaigns and webmail senders had lower reply rates (no figures) — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- Mailshake "State of Cold Email 2025": survey of 508 outbound professionals; "average reply rate is only 1–4%"; nearly half of senders don't track bounce rates; only 5% personalize every e-mail (linked to 2–3x better reply rates); 69% reported YoY performance decline (search snippet of PDF) — [Mailshake](https://mailshake.com/?p=16982); [Mailshake PDF](https://assets.mailshake.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/16091740/Cold-Email-Report-2025-Mailshake.pdf)
- Reply.io research on 712K first-step e-mails: benchmark averages of 31.69% open rate and 3.02% reply rate (date not confirmed) — [Reply.io](https://reply.io/reply-research-subject-lines/)
- Lavender cold e-mail benchmark (as of Feb 4, 2026): 231,818 most recent cold e-mails from ~50k inboxes on Lavender's Email Coach; excludes auto-replies, includes positive and negative replies. Segment examples: Operations persona — A-grade e-mails reach 5.4% reply (58% lift); Finance — only 6.1% of e-mails earn an A, A-grade lift 79% (highest); technical buyers 5.2% reply rate. Full table is an interactive widget (not captured) — [Lavender benchmark report](https://www.lavender.ai/blog/the-cold-email-benchmark-report). A third-party review quotes Lavender's benchmark as A-graded e-mails improving reply rate from 3.4% to 4.3% — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/lavender-ai/). Lavender's marketing claims 20.5% average reply rate across its users (Dec 2023) — [Demand Gen Report](https://demandgenreport.com/?p=8123)
- Backlinko/Pitchbox (OLDER DATA, last updated April 2019; link-building/PR outreach, not sales): 12M outreach e-mails; 8.5% received a response — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study)
- Open rates: Instantly advises tracking opens only per provider (to detect throttling), Hunter advises against focusing on open rate and found open-tracked campaigns reply less; Belkins' report page says "open rates lost their meaning" — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks); [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025); [Belkins](https://belkins.io/resources/b2b-cold-outreach-benchmarks-2025-report)
- Published cold-e-mail benchmarks vary by as much as 7.6x depending on methodology (EmailBison, comparing Instantly 3.43%, Saleshandy 3.7%, Belkins 0.45%) — [EmailBison](https://emailbison.com/blogs/cold-email-open-rates)

**Cold calling**
- Gong Labs, 300M+ cold calls: average rep connects 5.4% of dials (≈19 dials per conversation); top-quartile rep 13.3% (≈8 dials per conversation); conversation-to-meeting ("set") rate 4.6% average vs. 16.7% top quartile (>3x); modelled at 800 dials/month: ≈2 meetings/month (average) vs. 18 (top); cold calling nearly doubles e-mail reply rate even without a live connect: 3.44% vs. 1.81% — [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls)
- Gong cold-calling stats page: successful cold calls average 5:50 min vs. 3:14 for unsuccessful (search snippet) — [Gong cold calling page](https://www.gong.io/cold-calling)
- Cognism "State of Cold Calling 2025" (WHAM data; 2025 dataset described as "much bigger" than 2024; worldwide 41,936 connected calls → 27,513 conversations ≈65.6%): success rate (conversations → meeting booked) 4.82% in 2024, "almost half" in 2025; Cognism's success-rate article states 2.3% for 2025; industry average ≈2%; Cognism's own teams 6.7%; localised reps in new regions 6–8%. Average call duration 83 s (2024) → 93 s (2025); only ~10% of calls pass 2 minutes (Jason Bay) — [Cognism report](https://www.cognism.com/cold-calling-report-2025); [Cognism success rates](https://cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-success-rates); [Cognism statistics roundup, 204,000+ calls](https://www.cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-statistics)
- Cognism: ~3 call attempts per connection on average; 65.6% of connected calls become a live conversation; callbacks reach the prospect 26.85% of the time — [Cognism statistics](https://www.cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-statistics)
- Cognism diminishing returns: conversations drop 73% from call 1 to call 2 and a further 60% from call 2 to 3; 3 calls capture 93% of total conversations; 5 calls capture 98.6% — [Cognism report](https://www.cognism.com/cold-calling-report-2025)
- Cognism timing: Tuesday = most meetings booked; Friday good for conversations but among the worst for bookings; longest calls 10–11am, runner-up 2–3pm; dips at 7–9am, 12pm, 5pm — [Cognism report](https://www.cognism.com/cold-calling-report-2025)
- Mobile numbers: Jason Bay estimates pickup rates under 1% without mobile numbers and 5–10x higher with cell phones — [Cognism report](https://www.cognism.com/cold-calling-report-2025)
- Belkins 2025: 46,000+ call records across 8 client projects; calls produced 33.6% of all 2025 appointments vs. 50% from e-mail — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics)

**LinkedIn outreach**
- Belkins LinkedIn Outreach Study 2025 (Belkins: 14,077 contact records, 34 projects; Expandi: 15.1M outreach contacts, 11.5M connection requests, Jan–Dec 2025): overall acceptance 26% (Belkins own funnel 18.7%); reply rates by campaign type: Messenger 12.2%, Connector 7.9% (8.5M contacts), Open InMail 7.5% (13,600 contacts, directional), Builder 5.4%; global average reply 7.2%; by country: Netherlands 10.5%, Canada 7.1%, Australia 6.4%, UK 6.1%, US 6.0%; by seniority: HR/Talent 10.9%, C-level 7.0%, Founders 6.4%, Marketing & Finance <6%; by industry: VC/PE and Staffing ≈10.5%, Retail/Consumer Electronics/Luxury ≈4.2%; best month April 7.9%, worst September 6.3%; day-of-week range <1 pt (Thu/Mon ≈7.4%, Fri/Sat 6.9%) — [Belkins LinkedIn study](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study)
- Expandi H1/Q1 2026 report (70,130+ campaigns; survey of 2,200+ sellers): Connector acceptance 29.61%, Mobile Connector 22.32%, Builder 22%; January annual low 21.45%; average LinkedIn reply 10.3%; Messenger 16.86%, Event campaigns 14.21%, Inbound Visitors 13.4%, template-based 8.62%, Builder 7.22%; LinkedIn reply rates ≈101% higher than cold e-mail (5.1%); 66.96% of campaigns combine LinkedIn + e-mail; 71% of users send ≤50 requests/week; LinkedIn's limit cited as 80–100 requests/week — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040)
- Expandi 13.2M requests (May 2025–Apr 2026): average acceptance ≈28.5%; message reply 10.4%; replies to the note on the request itself ≈3.0% (fell 37% YoY) (search summary) — [Expandi benchmark tool](https://expandi.io/free-tools/benchmark/)
- Recruiting-specific (not comparable to B2B sales): 19.4% acceptance on 76,507 requests; 16.6% per-candidate reply across sequences — [Noon](https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/192-linkedin-connection-acceptance-rate-benchmarks)
- Channel preference survey (Hunter, 217 decision-makers): prefer cold e-mail 61% (71% US), LinkedIn 29%, cold call 10% — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)

### Inferences
- The 0.45–0.74% (per e-mail/contact) vs. 3.4–5.8% (per campaign/sequence or strong-sender) split is a denominator artefact, not a real disagreement: a 3–6-step sequence at ~0.5%/step and per-contact de-duplication lands near 2–4% per contact. Any benchmark must be quoted with its denominator.
- Backlinko's 8.5% is from 2019 link-building outreach and should not be used as a 2025 B2B sales benchmark.
- Across datasets, the phone has the highest engagement per reached prospect (18.6% connect per Belkins; 5.4% per dial per Gong) but the lowest volume; e-mail still sources the most meetings in absolute terms (50% at Belkins).

### Gaps
- Belkins' flagship 2025 benchmark PDF (16.5M e-mails) is gated; its open/positive-reply/meeting numbers could not be verified first-hand.
- No source gave a robust, platform-wide "positive reply rate" or "meetings per 1,000 e-mails" benchmark; only Belkins' ≈0.2% appointment rate per e-mail step is available.
- Cognism's 2025 exact dial count and the exact 2025 success rate on the report page (2.3% appears only in a separate article) could not be reconciled; it is unclear whether 2.3% is "almost half" of 4.82% or a rounding.
- Lavender's full benchmark table (reply rate by department/seniority/industry) is in a widget that could not be read.
- Gong's "28 million cold e-mails" 2025 analysis is only cited second-hand; the primary page was not found.

## Key Question 2 — Documented frameworks and what each prescribes

### Takeaway
The frameworks split into (a) message-construction formulas (AIDA, PAS, Lavender's scoring rules, Josh Braun's "poke the bear"/illumination question, Challenger teaching insight, Voss no-oriented questions), (b) research/targeting disciplines (3x3 research, trigger-based outreach, account-based multi-contact), and (c) activity/system disciplines (Fanatical Prospecting's 30-Day Rule, Law of Familiarity, 5-Step Telephone Framework; Salesloft/Yesware cadence structures). Most prescriptions are documented by their authors or vendors; quantitative proof is strongest for Lavender's rules (correlational, large N) and weakest for Braun/Voss/Challenger applied to cold e-mail.

### Cited Findings
- **Lavender e-mail scoring rules** (help-center "best practices", correlational across Lavender's dataset): 0–50-word e-mails receive 23% more replies; 3rd–7th-grade reading-level ("clarity") e-mails receive 1.66x more replies ("write so an elementary school student can understand"). Note: the help article URL returned HTTP 404 when fetched on 2026-10-23; figures are from the search snippet of that article — [Lavender help center (snippet)](https://help.lavender.ai/en/articles/6879935-lavender-best-practices); Lavender's benchmark defines "Lift" as % change from average reply rate to reply rate of e-mails scoring 90+ — [Lavender benchmark](https://www.lavender.ai/blog/the-cold-email-benchmark-report)
- **Josh Braun "Poke the Bear" / illumination questions**: "ditch the pitch, ask one sharp question that surfaces a real, invisible problem"; an "illumination question" is a neutral, non-pitchy question shining light on a problem the prospect may not know they have (example: asking whether their e-mails land in spam rather than pitching a deliverability fix); follow-up touches show the gap between "their world now and their world after" then ask a doubt-planting question — [11x guide to Braun's method](https://www.11x.ai/guides/josh-braun-cold-outreach-method); Braun's own script is described as six moves: permission, verification, context, problem, labelling, poke — [eChai listing of joshbraun.com resource](https://echai.ventures/gtm/from/joshbraun-com); Braun's course outline lists "Step 3: Poke The Bear" and question templates (content gated) — [Josh Braun Teachable](https://joshbraun.teachable.com/courses/poke/lectures/47070266)
- **Fanatical Prospecting (Jeb Blount, 2015 — older but still the standard reference)**: 30-Day Rule — prospecting done in any 30-day period pays off over the next 90 days; Law of Replacement — a rep with a 10% close rate must replace ten prospects for every deal closed; Law of Familiarity — the more familiar the prospect is with you/your brand the likelier they respond, with cold prospects requiring as many as 20–50 touches; 5-Step Telephone Framework — (1) get attention using the prospect's name, (2) identify yourself, (3) state why you are calling, (4) bridge with a "because" tied to their situation, (5) ask for what you want and stop talking; block the first hours of each morning for phone prospecting — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/fanatical-prospecting/summary/); publisher lists 30-Day Rule, Law of Replacement, Law of Familiarity, 5 Cs of Social Selling, 5-Step Telephone Framework as core topics — [Porchlight Books](https://www.porchlightbooks.com/products/fanatical-prospecting-jeb-blount-9781119144755)
- **3x3 research**: spend ~3 minutes to find 3 key pieces of information about a prospect (up to three trigger events/data points), then use them to dictate the opening line and CTA; check ICP fit before the outbound call — [Regie.ai](https://regie.ai/blog/3-x-3-research-101); variant: 1 fact each on the person, the company, and the industry, in 3 minutes — [Daily Sales Rep](https://dailysalesrep.substack.com/p/personalize-cold-outreach-scale)
- **Trigger-based outreach**: triggers are verifiable events (funding round, executive departure, new office, hiring) distinct from intent data; act within 24–48 hours because signals decay; stacking multiple signals beats a single one; naming the event without a reason to care "reads as generic anyway"; link the trigger to the value of the solution rather than a congratulatory note — [Autobound](https://www.autobound.ai/guides/sales-trigger-events); [Cleverly](https://www.cleverly.co/blog/trigger-based-cold-email); [Salesforge](https://www.salesforge.ai/blog/5-trigger-based-email-examples-for-cold-outreach). Vendor claims of "3–5x" (Autobound) and "8x" (Salesforge) reply lifts are unverified.
- **Challenger approach applied to prospecting**: reps who win complex deals "teach the buyer something new about their own problem" rather than build the best relationship; the prospecting application is a "teaching opener" — a counter-intuitive observation about the buyer's market/operations that reframes a problem — [Pulse RevOps summary of The Challenger Sale](https://pulserevops.com/sales-book-summaries/bs0378); a vendor blog (dated "2027", discount its figures) claims Relationship-Builder-style friendly openers lower response in complex sales — [Mailshake](https://mailshake.com/?p=15485)
- **Chris Voss-style questions**: frame questions in the negative to elicit a "no" (e.g., "Is now a bad time?") rather than asking for a yes; described as producing more engaged responses, with no data cited — [Triple Session](https://triplesession.com/session/4-questions-that-will-stop-them-from-ghosting-you)
- **Account-based / multi-contact outreach**: e-mailing more than one contact at an organisation improved response 93% vs. a single contact; diminishing at 5+ contacts; multi-contact + multi-message = 160% higher response than single e-mail to single contact (2019 data, link-building outreach) — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study)
- **Salesloft cadence structure** (Data Science team, 200M interactions; 3.4M cadence sample): single-channel cadences get much lower response rates — e-mail-only 77% lower, call-only 91% lower than multichannel; execute a high number of touches in the first 10 business days with gradually widening gaps; 80% of the top 100 cadences started with a call followed by an e-mail (undated eBook, likely pre-2020) — [Salesloft eBook PDF](https://pages.salesloft.com/rs/432-WAJ-793/images/33%20Tips%20for%20Optimizing%20B2B%20Sales%20Emails%20eBook.pdf)
- **Yesware cadence** (33M e-mails; older dataset): prescribes channel-specific day gaps (e.g., second touch: wait one day and e-mail) — [Yesware](https://www.yesware.com/blog/sales-cadence/)
- **Gong top-rep practices (qualitative)**: prioritise direct dials, mark bad numbers, prioritise prospects with a current problem (e.g., upcoming comp review, recent fundraise) over generic ICP matches — [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls)
- **Belkins subject-line prescriptions** (5.5M e-mails, 2024): 2–4 words, frame as a question, include a CTA, personalise with name/company/location/event, avoid generic greetings/urgency/jargon — [Belkins subject lines](https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics)

### Inferences
- The modern consensus formula visible across Lavender, Instantly, Hunter and Smartlead is: <50–80 words, elementary reading level, one observation tied to a trigger, one question/CTA, 3–5 steps. AIDA/PAS are not evidenced by any 2023–2026 dataset found; they survive as copywriting mnemonics, not measured methods.
- Braun's "poke the bear", Voss's no-oriented question and Challenger's teaching opener are variations of the same mechanism (ask/teach instead of pitch); none has a published A/B dataset.

### Gaps
- No primary text from Josh Braun, Chris Voss, or the Challenger authors specifically on cold e-mail was accessible; descriptions are second-hand.
- AIDA and PAS: no 2023–2026 quantitative evidence found for cold e-mail; not covered beyond their status as classic copy formulas.
- Kyle Coleman, Aaron Ross (Predictable Revenue / "Cold Calling 2.0" referral e-mail) and Will Allred's individual articles were not located within the call budget.
- The Salesloft "Cadence Optimization Report 2025 / 47M contacts / 22–38% cumulative reply" cited by a 2026 blog could not be found and should be treated as unverified — [Zeliq](https://www.zeliq.com/blog/sales-follow-up)

## Key Question 3 — Personalization depth, message length, follow-ups, send times, multichannel sequencing

### Takeaway
Data consistently rewards short e-mails (<50–80 words), 3–5 total steps with the third step the most productive, and relevance/segment or trigger-level personalization over superficial first lines; on LinkedIn a personalised note slightly lowers acceptance but raises replies ~55%; send-time effects are small (sub-1-point day-of-week ranges) except an early-morning edge; multichannel (call + e-mail + LinkedIn) is universally reported to outperform single-channel, with Gong's 3.44% vs 1.81% e-mail-reply lift from calling the cleanest number.

### Cited Findings

**Personalization**
- Belkins subject-line personalization (5.5M e-mails, 2024): open rate 46% with vs. 35% without (+31%); reply rate 7% with vs. 3% without (+133%) — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics)
- Belkins LinkedIn 2025: connection request with personalised note 25.3% acceptance vs. 27.6% without, but reply rate among accepters 8.2% vs. 5.3% (+55%); AI-assisted messaging 7.3% total reply vs. 7.0% without (AI: 3,431 campaigns/389,925 contacts; non-AI: 62,103 campaigns/13.9M contacts) — [Belkins LinkedIn study](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study)
- Smartlead 2026: typical personalization rate 75%; senders cluster at 0% or 100%, rarely in between (no reply-rate split given) — [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks)
- Smartlead claim (unverified against underlying data): signal-based personalization drives 56% more replies than generic; name-and-company-only adds 8% — [Smartlead blog via search](https://www.smartlead.ai/blog/email-optimization-best-practices)
- Hunter survey (217 decision-makers): reasons for not responding — lack of relevancy 71%, impersonality 43%, lack of trust 36%; 67% don't mind AI use in cold e-mails — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- Mailshake survey (508 pros): only 5% personalize every e-mail; those who do see 2–3x higher engagement — [Mailshake](https://mailshake.com/?p=16982)
- Instantly: recommends "one line showing you did the research" plus event triggers (hiring, funding, tech changes); multi-point personalization associated with the 10–20%+ band — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)
- Lavender (second-hand, 2.69M e-mails): personalized e-mails 13x more effective than automated; a personalized P.S. +35% replies (newsletter citation, unverified) — [Salesflo Substack](https://salesflo.substack.com/p/66-8-ways-to-use-theps-to-increase)
- Backlinko 2019: personalized subject lines +30.5% response; personalized bodies +32.7% — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study)
- Expandi survey: 53.71% of users personalize, 29.19% use AI, 17.10% do not personalize — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040)

**Message length**
- Smartlead 2026: first e-mails under 50 words = 1.47% reply (68 contacts/reply) vs. 100–149 words = 97 contacts/reply — [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks)
- Lavender: 0–50 words +23% replies; 3rd–7th grade reading level 1.66x (snippet; page 404) — [Lavender help](https://help.lavender.ai/en/articles/6879935-lavender-best-practices)
- Hunter: word count vs reply correlation −0.09 (weak); recommends 40–60 words plain text; images make no statistically significant difference — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- Instantly: 50–125 words (citing Boomerang data, no N); report cites <80 words as optimal — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks); [Instantly 2026](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sales-agent-benchmarks-2026-the-complete-performance-report/)
- Gong (second-hand, "28M cold e-mails, 2025"): ≤100 words and 3–4 sentences replied best — [LeadHaste citing Gong](https://leadhaste.com/blog/cold-email-subject-lines-2026)
- LinkedIn: messages under 400 characters perform 22% above average (vendor roundup) — [search summary, Expandi/others](https://expandi.io/?p=14040)

**Subject lines**
- Belkins (5.5M, 2024): open rate 2–4 words 46%; 1 word 38%; >7 words 39%; >9 words 35%; 10 words 34%; question-form 44–46%; CTA 44.6%; capitalisation differences ≤2 pts; numbers no effect (27% vs 28%) — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics)
- Backlinko 2019: ideal 36–50 characters; long subject lines beat short by 32.7% (summary says 24.6%, page internally inconsistent) — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study). Contradiction: Belkins favours 2–4 words (~15–25 chars) while Backlinko favours 36–50 chars; another roundup says two large studies found no correlation between subject length and opens — [LeadHaste](https://leadhaste.com/blog/cold-email-subject-lines-2026)

**Follow-ups: number and spacing**
- Belkins 2025 (7.5M): recommend 3–5 e-mail steps; reply rates fall sharply after step 5; step 3 most productive for meetings; steps 2–6 = 58.6% of replies — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics)
- Smartlead 2026: typical sequence 3 e-mails; bottom 10% send only one; e-mail 1 = 1.18% reply (85 contacts/reply) vs. e-mail 7 = 0.33% (303 contacts/reply); weakest senders rely on follow-ups long after the first send — [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks)
- Instantly: 1–3 follow-ups over 7–14 days; first follow-up adds 40–50% more replies vs. e-mail 1 alone; founder example day 3 and day 7; 2026 report says 4–7 steps optimal and ~58% of replies from e-mail 1 — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks); [Instantly 2026](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sales-agent-benchmarks-2026-the-complete-performance-report/)
- Hunter 2024 data: with follow-ups 4.9% vs 3% without; unsubscribe rates grow after the third follow-up; 48% of recipients appreciate follow-ups, 27% don't mind, 25% find them annoying; excessive follow-ups are a main driver of spam complaints — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- Belkins (second-hand): one follow-up increases reply rate up to 49% — [Komo glossary citing Belkins](https://komo.ai/glossary/sales-cadence); Backlinko 2019: one follow-up +65.8%, 3+ messages best — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study)
- Cold-call attempts: 3 attempts capture 93% of conversations, 5 capture 98.6% (Cognism) — [Cognism](https://www.cognism.com/cold-calling-report-2025)
- LinkedIn follow-ups (Expandi): first follow-up −0.6% responses vs none; second follow-up +4.05%; third+ up to ~1%, diminishing — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040); Builder campaigns get almost all replies from subsequent actions (0.06% first vs 5.3% subsequent) — [Belkins LinkedIn](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study)
- Disagreement: Belkins 2025 says step 1 = 41.4% of replies; Instantly 2026 says ~58% from e-mail 1; Belkins 2024 (second-hand) says one-e-mail sequences had the highest per-e-mail reply rate (8.4%) — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics); [Instantly 2026](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-sales-agent-benchmarks-2026-the-complete-performance-report/)

**Send times**
- Smartlead 2026: best hour ≈7am recipient time, 1.34% reply (75 contacts/reply) vs ≈120 in the afternoon; day-of-week 0.82–0.91% reply (Wednesday 110 contacts/reply, Saturday 122); 64% of replies within 1 hour, 88.5% within 24 h, median ~10 minutes — [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks)
- Hunter: recipients report indifference to timing — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- Backlinko 2019: Wednesday best, Saturday worst (7.99% vs 6%); weekdays +23.3% vs weekends; author calls the effect small — [Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/email-outreach-study)
- LinkedIn: 63% of acceptances arrive within 24 hours (vendor roundup); January is the annual low for acceptance (21.45%) and reply (6.47%) at Expandi; April high/September low at Belkins — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040); [Belkins LinkedIn](https://belkins.io/blog/linkedin-outreach-study)
- Calls: Tuesday best for bookings; 10–11am longest calls (Cognism) — [Cognism](https://www.cognism.com/cold-calling-report-2025)

**Multichannel sequencing**
- Gong: adding cold calls lifts e-mail reply rate from 1.81% to 3.44% even without a live connect — [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-hidden-power-of-cold-calling-insights-from-300m-calls)
- Salesloft (200M interactions): e-mail-only cadences 77% lower response, call-only 91% lower than multichannel; front-load touches in the first 10 business days; 80% of top-100 cadences start call-then-e-mail — [Salesloft eBook](https://pages.salesloft.com/rs/432-WAJ-793/images/33%20Tips%20for%20Optimizing%20B2B%20Sales%20Emails%20eBook.pdf)
- Belkins 2025 channel mix of appointments: e-mail 50%, calls 33.6% — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics)
- Expandi: 66.96% of LinkedIn campaigns also use e-mail; LinkedIn reply 10.3% vs e-mail 5.1% — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040)
- Recruiting data (Noon): LinkedIn-first sequences 18.8% vs e-mail-first 16.4% reply — [Noon](https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/188-recruiting-outreach-benchmarks-2026)
- Quickmail executive claim: combining e-mail + LinkedIn ≈50% more replies than either alone (no dataset) — [Sproutworth](https://www.sproutworth.com/multichannel-cold-outreach/); a "287%" multichannel lift circulates without a primary study — [Landbase](https://www.landbase.com/blog/multi-channel-outreach-statistics)

### Inferences
- The LinkedIn note finding (lower acceptance, higher replies) suggests personalization filters for fit rather than raising raw volume — the same pattern as Hunter's small-campaign (5.8%) vs. large-campaign (2.1%) gap.
- Step-3 dominance in Belkins' meeting data plus Smartlead's decline by e-mail 7 implies the optimum is 3–5 steps over ~2 weeks; beyond that, complaint risk (Hunter) outweighs marginal replies.
- Send-time optimisation is worth less than a percentage point in every dataset; list quality and copy dominate.

### Gaps
- No dataset isolates "first-line personalization" vs. "segment-level relevance" with a controlled comparison; the Smartlead 56%/8% claim is the closest and is unverified.
- Exact day-spacing between e-mail follow-ups is prescriptive (Instantly: day 3/day 7) rather than measured in any 2025–2026 dataset found.
- No primary lemlist multichannel study was found; only customer case studies.

## Key Question 4 — Deliverability and technical prerequisites (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Since February 2024 Google and Yahoo enforce authentication (SPF/DKIM for everyone; SPF + DKIM + DMARC with From-domain alignment for bulk senders >5,000 msgs/day), spam-complaint rates below 0.3% (target <0.1%), valid forward/reverse DNS, TLS, RFC 5322 compliance and one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058, honoured within 2 days at Yahoo); platform data adds practical limits of ~14–30 e-mails per mailbox per day (50 as a ceiling), bounce <2%, and warm-up before sending.

### Cited Findings
- Google (support.google.com/mail/answer/81126): all senders since Feb 1, 2024 — SPF or DKIM; DKIM key ≥1024 bits (2048 recommended); TLS; valid forward and reverse DNS (sending IP must match PTR hostname which must have a matching A/AAAA record); spam rate in Postmaster Tools below 0.3%; RFC 5322 formatting; no Gmail From: impersonation. Bulk senders (>5,000 msgs/day to Gmail): SPF and DKIM both required; DMARC required (policy may be p=none); From: domain must align with SPF or DKIM domain; spam rate below 0.30%, recommended below 0.10%, reaching 0.30% "leads to increased spam classification"; one-click unsubscribe via `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` + `List-Unsubscribe` HTTPS URL for marketing/subscribed messages, plus a visible unsubscribe link; Gmail "will begin using a DMARC quarantine enforcement policy" (no date on page) — [Google Email sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126)
- Yahoo (senders.yahooinc.com): enforcement from February 2024, rolled out gradually through H1 2024. All senders: SPF or DKIM at minimum; spam rate below 0.3%; valid forward/reverse DNS; RFC 5321/5322. Bulk senders: both SPF and DKIM; DMARC policy at least p=none and DMARC must pass; relaxed alignment acceptable; From: aligned with SPF or DKIM domain; List-Unsubscribe header with one-click (RFC 8058 POST "highly recommended", mailto acceptable) plus visible link; "Honor unsubscribes within 2 days"; spam rate "calculated in our system based on mail delivered to the inbox"; recommends DKIM ≥1024-bit, ARC if forwarding, enrolling in Complaint Feedback Loop for all DKIM domains, meaningful non-generic PTR records. Page does not define "bulk sender" numerically — [Yahoo Sender Best Practices](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/)
- Secondary sources state the 5,000/day threshold applies to Yahoo too and that Yahoo treats >0.1% as a warning (single-source, unconfirmed) — [Warmy](https://www.warmy.io/guides/google-yahoo-sender-requirements/); [Sendmarc](https://sendmarc.com/dmarc/yahoo-dmarc-requirements/)
- Apollo: Gmail tightened enforcement against bulk senders in November 2025 (domains sending ~5,000 daily messages to personal Gmail accounts) and Microsoft added authentication requirements for Outlook.com; Validity 2024 figures cited: ~6.7% of messages land in spam and 9.8% go missing — [Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/how-do-i-cut-my-cold-email-bounce-rate-and-keep-messages-out-of-spam-while-prospecting). Sendmarc mentions stricter DMARC rules from November 2025 (not confirmed against a primary source) — [Sendmarc](https://sendmarc.com/dmarc/google-dmarc-requirements/)
- Smartlead 2026 operating data: verified senders (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) need 108 contacts per reply vs 120 unverified (~11% more work without authentication); by provider Gmail 90, Outlook 120, custom servers 179 contacts per reply; warm-up inbox placement ≈99%; typical 8.0 days from setup to first campaign (down from 9.4); typical mailbox volume 13.9 e-mails/day, heaviest 10% ≈42, 50 cited as the safe limit; typical account 20 mailboxes; typical campaign 499 contacts; bounce danger line 2% — [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks)
- Instantly: max 30 sends per inbox per day, ramped gradually; bounce <2%; spam complaints "far below 0.3%"; start with two warmed inboxes — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)
- Hunter: Outlook with custom domain highest reply (5.9%); webmail (free) sender addresses "extremely low" reply (<100 campaigns, caveated); open-tracking lowers replies — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- Snov.io (10M+ e-mails): bounce rates spike during days 20–50 of domain warm-up — [Snov.io](https://snov.io/blog/what-affects-email-deliverability-snovio-report/)
- Mailshake: nearly half of senders don't track bounce rates or domain reputation — [Mailshake](https://mailshake.com/?p=16982)
- LinkedIn volume limits: 80–100 connection requests/week cited; 71% of Expandi users send ≤50/week — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040)

### Inferences
- Cold outbound at any meaningful volume almost always exceeds 5,000/day in aggregate only across many domains, which is why practitioners split sends across secondary domains and 20+ mailboxes at 14–30/day each (Smartlead/Instantly); but each sending domain still needs SPF+DKIM+DMARC with alignment because the <0.3% complaint rule applies to all senders.
- One-click unsubscribe is formally required for "marketing and subscribed" messages; the sources do not settle whether one-to-one cold sales e-mail must carry it, but Instantly lists its absence among causes of low reply rates.

### Gaps
- Google's page gives no date for DMARC quarantine enforcement and no unsubscribe-processing SLA; Yahoo's gives no numeric bulk-sender threshold.
- No primary Microsoft/Outlook.com requirements page was fetched.
- No 2025–2026 inbox-placement benchmark for cold e-mail specifically (Validity figure is 2024 and general).

## Key Question 5 — Why cold outreach fails, according to the data

### Takeaway
The datasets point to five failure causes in rough order: (1) irrelevance to the recipient (71% of decision-makers' stated reason), (2) deliverability/infrastructure (authentication, reputation, bounces >2%, spam placement that is invisible to the sender), (3) poor list quality and over-broad targeting (large campaigns reply at a third the rate of small ones), (4) too long/too many asks/too many follow-ups (complaints rise after the third follow-up), and (5) no follow-up or single-channel execution.

### Cited Findings
- Hunter survey (217 decision-makers, 2025): non-response reasons — lack of relevancy 71%, impersonality 43%, lack of trust 36%; 20% never receive a relevant cold e-mail; only 24% receive a valuable one at least weekly; excessive follow-ups are a main driver of spam complaints; generic "spray and pray" named as a cause — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- Hunter: campaign size — ≤50 recipients 5.8% vs 1,000+ recipients 2.1% reply — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- Instantly's list of causes of low reply rates: landing in spam (deliverability/reputation), missing SPF/DKIM/DMARC, high complaints, no one-click unsubscribe, unverified contacts/bounces >2%, broad ICP/low intent, new domain, long e-mails, multiple asks in the first touch, no follow-ups, and inconsistent definitions of "cold" across studies — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)
- Smartlead 2026 traits of the weakest senders: bounce rates above 2%, single-e-mail sequences, heavy reliance on late follow-ups; worst quartile bounces ≥3.21% — [Smartlead](https://www.smartlead.ai/benchmarks)
- Mailshake 2025 (508 pros): 69% report YoY performance decline, attributed to spam filtering and decision-maker avoidance of AI-written content; nearly half don't track bounce rates; only 5% personalize every e-mail — [Mailshake](https://mailshake.com/?p=16982); [Mailshake PDF](https://assets.mailshake.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/16091740/Cold-Email-Report-2025-Mailshake.pdf). Conflict: Hunter finds 67% of recipients don't mind AI use — [Hunter](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- Apollo: a message can be accepted by the mail server and never reach a human; hard bounces (invalid addresses) and spam placement (authentication, engagement, copy) have different causes but both look like zero replies — [Apollo](https://www.apollo.io/insights/how-do-i-cut-my-cold-email-bounce-rate-and-keep-messages-out-of-spam-while-prospecting)
- Folderly: usual culprits are poor SPF/DKIM/DMARC, weak reputation (high bounces, low engagement), aggressive HTML formatting, list-quality issues; bounce >3% in recent campaigns as a warning sign — [Folderly](https://folderly.com/blog/cold-email-deliverability)
- Belkins report page (qualitative): e-mail "could no longer carry the load", "open rates lost their meaning", spam filters "got smarter" — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/resources/b2b-cold-outreach-benchmarks-2025-report)
- Cold-call failure: top 5 objections (WHAM/Cognism) — "I'm not interested", "I don't have the problem you're describing", "I already have a solution", "I'm busy, call back", "Could you email me instead?"; only ~10% of calls pass 2 minutes; pickup <1% without mobile numbers — [Cognism](https://www.cognism.com/cold-calling-report-2025)
- Cold-call success collapse: Cognism success rate 4.82% (2024) → 2.3% (2025) — [Cognism success rates](https://cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-success-rates)
- Subject-line failure patterns: marketing jargon, generic greetings and urgency terms open below 36% vs 46% for 2–4-word personalised lines — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/b2b-cold-email-subject-line-statistics)
- Single-channel execution: e-mail-only cadences 77% lower response, call-only 91% lower (Salesloft, older) — [Salesloft eBook](https://pages.salesloft.com/rs/432-WAJ-793/images/33%20Tips%20for%20Optimizing%20B2B%20Sales%20Emails%20eBook.pdf)
- Seasonality as a hidden failure cause: LinkedIn January acceptance 21.45% (−33%) and reply 6.47%, the yearly low, while January has the highest campaign volume — [Expandi](https://expandi.io/?p=14040)

### Inferences
- Because spam placement is invisible (accepted but not delivered to inbox) and half of senders do not track bounces, a large share of "copy" failures are probably infrastructure failures misdiagnosed.
- The decline in both e-mail (Belkins 7% → 5.1%; Instantly 5% → 3.43%) and call (4.82% → 2.3%) benchmarks between 2024 and 2025 across independent vendors suggests channel saturation rather than a single vendor artefact.

### Gaps
- No independent 2025–2026 study ranks the causes of cold-outreach failure quantitatively; all rankings are vendor surveys or vendor diagnostics.
- Exact objection frequencies (Cognism) appear only in a chart and could not be extracted.
