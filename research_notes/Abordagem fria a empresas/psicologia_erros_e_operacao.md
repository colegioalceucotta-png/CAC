# Cold outreach to businesses: behavioral science, documented failure modes, and the operating system

Scope note: findings are tagged **[PEER-REVIEWED]** (journal-published experiments/meta-analyses), **[VENDOR]** (sales-tool companies analysing their own customers' data; observational, commercially motivated, usually no controls for list quality), **[SURVEY]** (self-report), or **[PRACTITIONER]** (books/blogs without data). Sample sizes are given wherever the source states them. Most industry data is 2020-2026; classic experiments are older. Many "classic" percentages circulate only through secondary summaries; where I could not reach the original paper I say so.

---

## KQ1. Which experiments (Cialdini, Langer, Bohns, Epley & Schroeder, Flynn & Lake, etc.) are relevant to cold requests, and what did they find quantitatively?

### Takeaway
The single most robust, directly relevant finding is Bohns/Flynn's: requesters systematically underestimate how often strangers will say yes (by roughly half, across 14,000+ strangers), and recipients appreciate being contacted far more than senders expect (Liu et al., 5,900+ participants). The compliance "techniques" (because-justification, foot-in-the-door, but-you-are-free, pique/curiosity, similarity, local social proof, advice-seeking) all have experimental support, but effect sizes shrink in replications/meta-analyses, so they should be treated as modest multipliers on a relevant, well-targeted request, not as substitutes for relevance.

### Cited Findings

**Underestimating compliance / the psychology of asking**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Flynn & Lake (Bohns) 2008, JPSP, "If you need help, just ask": founding paper of the "underestimating compliance" line; Vanessa Lake later published as Bohns — [Bohns CV, Cornell ILR](https://archive.ilr.cornell.edu/sites/default/files/faculty/vitas/VBohns_CV_August2023.pdf)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Bohns 2016 review (Current Directions in Psychological Science): across studies in which participants approached more than 14,000 strangers, requesters predicted about 48% fewer "yeses" than they actually got; mechanism proposed is that requesters underweight how awkward it is for the target to refuse — [ScienceBlog summary](https://scienceblog.com/g-across-studies-with-more-than-14-000-strangers-people-underestimated-how-often-a-direct-request-would-get-a-yes-by-about-half-and-the-same-blind-spot-showed-up-when-the-request-was-to-commit-a-small/); [Cornell eCommons record](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/entities/publication/2c50b460-fb5c-4490-9047-9bb02b9f6f25)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Bohns, Roghanizad & Xu 2014 (PSPB): the same blind spot holds for unethical requests; ~64% of strangers agreed to write "pickle" in a library book vs. a predicted 28% — [Daily Maverick excerpt of Bohns](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2021-09-06-the-reasons-youre-more-persuasive-face-to-face-than-on-email/)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Roghanizad & Bohns 2016/2017, Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, "Ask in person: You're less persuasive than you think over email": 45 participants each asked 10 strangers to complete a survey, half by email and half face-to-face, same script. Face-to-face requests were ~34x more effective (ratio of success rates); both groups had predicted ~50% success beforehand, so emailers grossly overestimated email. Second study supported the explanation that recipients treat unknown emails as suspicious and that nonverbal cues matter. Limits: strangers only, single survey task — [University of Waterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/management-science-engineering/news/face-face-request-34-times-more-successful-email); [Mental Floss](https://www.mentalfloss.com/science/psychology/want-something-asking-person-34-times-more-effective-email); [HubSpot summary](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/make-requests-over-email-or-in-person); [Mannheim citation incl. DOI 10.1016/j.jesp.2016.10.002](https://www.bwl.uni-mannheim.de/news/frag-lieber-persoenlich/)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Liu, Rim, Min & Min 2022, JPSP (the "reaching out" study): 13 experiments, 5,900+ participants incl. three field experiments. Initiators consistently underestimated how much recipients appreciated being reached out to; the gap was largest when the contact was unexpected and when the tie was weak — exactly the cold-contact situation. Note: the task brief attributed this to Epley & Schroeder; the "appreciation of reaching out" result is Liu et al. (Epley & Schroeder's related work is on people underestimating how pleasant talking to strangers is, "Mistakenly seeking solitude", 2014) — [APA press release](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2022/07/friends-enjoy-being-reached); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/study-identifies-a-robust-tendency-to-underestimate-how-much-others-appreciate-being-reached-out-to); [KU Business](https://business.ku.edu/people-underestimate-surprising-impact-reaching-out-study-finds)

**"Because" justification (Langer)**
- [PEER-REVIEWED, numbers via secondary] Langer, Blank & Chanowitz 1978, JPSP, "The mindlessness of ostensibly thoughtful action": copy-machine queue, small request (5 pages). No reason: 60% complied; real reason ("because I'm in a rush"): 94%; placebic reason ("because I have to make copies"): 93%. With the large request (20 pages) the placebic reason lost its power and only the real reason helped (figures for the large-request condition were not retrievable from the sources found). Percentages come from popular write-ups, not the original paper — [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/ca/blog/brain-wise/201310/the-power-of-the-word-because-to-get-people-to-do-stuff); [James Clear](https://jamesclear.com/copy-machine-study)
- [VENDOR] Convergent industry evidence: Gong's analysis of cold calls found that stating "the reason for my call is..." raises success 2.1x — [Gong cold-call stats](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats)

**Commitment/consistency: foot-in-the-door**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Freedman & Fraser 1966: homeowners who had first accepted a small "Be a safe driver" sign later accepted the large ugly "Drive Carefully" lawn sign at 76% vs 17% for the direct-request control; in the soap-survey experiment, 52.8% agreed to a 2-hour home inventory after a prior short survey vs 22.2% cold. Caveat: meta-analytic effect of FITD is modest (r ≈ 0.17) — [SAGE encyclopedia entry](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/socialpsychology/chpt/footinthedoor-technique); [Yu-kai Chou summary](https://yukaichou.com/behavioral-analysis/foot-in-the-door-freedman-fraser-small-commitment/); [replication note](https://www.atticusli.com/replication-crisis/foot-in-the-door/)

**Autonomy / "but you are free" (BYAF)**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Carpenter 2013 meta-analysis: 42 studies; adding "but you are free to refuse" roughly doubled compliance with small requests — [Carpenter quoted, Muddy River News](https://muddyrivernews.com/communities/wiu-professor-chris-carpenter-receives-prestigious-2025-meta-analysis-award/20250717085800/)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Fillon et al. 2023 pre-registered re-meta-analysis (Meta-Psychology): 52 experiments, overall g = 0.44 (medium), but among only the 7 low-risk-of-bias studies the effect was g = 0.11 and non-significant; most recent studies underpowered — [Meta-Psychology](https://open.lnu.se/index.php/metapsychology/article/view/2640)

**Legitimizing small asks ("even a penny helps")**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Cialdini & Schroeder 1976, JPSP: adding "even a penny will help" to a door-to-door charity ask significantly increased the proportion who donated without reducing average gift size. The 50% vs 28.6% figures often quoted were NOT confirmed in sources found. Later replications are mixed: a 2000 study found no effect; a meta-analysis of 10 reports / 2,054 participants found no significant difference in total donations — [SAGE citation](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.2466/pr0.2000.86.2.551); [EA Forum review comment](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/WTpwDW8dHWzfiaumx/review-what-works-to-promote-charitable-donations/comment/inQ6YvHuNai75EufB)

**Curiosity gap / pattern interrupt (pique technique)**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Loewenstein 1994, Psychological Bulletin, information-gap theory: curiosity is "cognitively induced deprivation" from perceiving a gap in knowledge; requires some prior knowledge (one is not curious about what one knows nothing about) — [Stafforini summary w/ abstract](https://stafforini.com/works/loewenstein-1994-psychology-curiosity-review/)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Santos, Leve & Pratkanis 1994 (J. Applied Social Psychology), "pique technique": panhandlers asking for an odd amount ("17 cents" / "37 cents") got more compliance than those asking for "a quarter"/"any change" because the unusual request disrupts the automatic refusal script — [BPS Research Digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/pique-persuasion-technique-plays-our-curiosity-and-its-surprisingly-effective)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Lee & Feeley 2017 meta-analysis of the pique technique: 17 comparisons, overall r = .27; stronger when amount requested is small and when a reason is included; some studies null or negative — [Buffalo research record](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-the-pique-technique-of-compliance/)
- [VENDOR] Industry parallel: Gong, 90,380 cold calls, opening with "How've you been?" had a 10.01% success rate vs 1.5% baseline (6.6x); Gong frames it as a pattern interrupt — [Gong opening lines](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-opening-lines)

**Liking / incidental similarity**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Burger, Messian, Patel, del Prado & Anderson 2004 (PSPB), "What a coincidence!": four studies; participants complied more with a request when they believed they shared a birthday, first name, or fingerprint type with the requester; effect disappeared when similarity was common or came from someone other than the requester. The 62% vs 34% figures often attributed to this paper were not confirmed; 34% is from Finch & Cialdini's shared-birthday study — [Burger publication list](https://www.scu.edu/cas/psychology/emeritus/jerry-burger/jerry-burger---list-of-publications/); [PsyBlog on fleeting attraction](https://www.spring.org.uk/?p=12440)

**Social proof, especially local ("provincial") norms**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Goldstein, Cialdini & Griskevicius 2008 (J. Consumer Research), hotel towels: Exp. 1 (1,058 reuse opportunities) descriptive-norm card ("majority of guests reuse") beat the standard environmental card; Exp. 2 (five conditions) a same-room norm ("75% of guests who stayed in this room reused") beat the general norm and the identity-based ("fellow citizens") norm. The commonly quoted 35.1% / 44.1% / 49.3% figures were not retrievable from the summaries found — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/08/080822160338.htm); [AcaWiki summary](https://acawiki.org/A_Room_with_a_Viewpoint:_Using_Social_Norms_to_Motivate_Environmental_Conservation_in_Hotels)

**Reciprocity**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Regan 1971 (JPSP), "Coke" study: a confederate who brought the participant an unrequested soda later sold them more raffle tickets; once a favour had been given, liking for the confederate no longer predicted purchases (obligation overrode liking). Magnitude varies by summary ("twice as many" tickets; Cialdini describes a ~500% return) — [Wikipedia: Reciprocity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocity_(social_psychology))

**Pratfall / vulnerability**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Aronson, Willerman & Floyd 1966 (Psychonomic Science): 48 male undergraduates; a coffee-spill blunder INCREASED liking for a highly competent quiz contestant but DECREASED it for an average one. Effect conditional on prior competence; single scenario; male-only sample — [Explore Psychology](https://www.explorepsychology.com/pratfall-effect/); [PsyBlog](https://spring.org.uk/?p=59509)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Bruk, Scholl & Bless 2018 (JPSP 115(2):192-205), "beautiful mess effect": seven studies; people evaluate others' displays of vulnerability (asking for help, admitting mistakes) more positively than their own, explained via construal level; mostly German students and written scenarios — [Mannheim record](https://www-dev.mzes.uni-mannheim.de/en/publications/details/beautiful-mess-effect-self-other-differences-in-evaluation-of-showing-vulnerability); [ScienceBlog](https://scienceblog.com/g-beautiful-mess-effect-vulnerability-status/)

**Advice-seeking as an ask**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Brooks, Gino & Schweitzer 2015, Management Science 61(6): people fear asking for advice looks incompetent, but advisers rated advice-seekers as MORE competent; brainteaser experiment n = 170 randomly assigned; effect stronger for hard tasks, when advice is sought from the rater personally, and from experts; no boost to warmth — [MIT Sloan Review](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/?p=35844); [Scandinavia Standard summary](https://www.scandinaviastandard.com/g-people-believe-that-asking-for-advice-makes-them-look-incompetent-and-in-one-set-of-experiments-the-advisers-rated-them-as-more-competent-instead-though-only-on-hard-tasks-and-only-within-the-advise/)

**Personalization (academic field experiment)**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Sahni, Wheeler & Chintagunta 2018, Marketing Science 37:236-258: randomized field experiments with three organizations (a test-prep company, MercadoLibre, Stanford), millions of emails; adding recipient's name to the subject line raised opens ~20% (9.05% to 10.80%), sales leads ~31% (0.39% to 0.51%) and cut unsubscribes ~17% — [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/open-name-why-personalized-email-subject-lines-work); [RePEc](https://econpapers.repec.org/paper/eclstabus/3409.htm)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Defau & Zauner 2023, Marketing Letters: replication found NO positive effect of first name in subject line on opens or clicks (surname did help); authors suggest wear-out as the tactic became common — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/mktlet/v34y2023i4d10.1007_s11002-023-09701-7.html)

**Loss aversion and door-in-the-face**
- No cold-outreach-specific experiments found for loss framing in B2B cold messages; the closest vendor datum is Gong's finding that ROI/multiplier/percentage language in cold emails LOWERS success (see KQ2). Door-in-the-face (Cialdini 1975) was not researched in this pass (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The two "meta" findings (Bohns: you underestimate yes-rates; Liu: recipients appreciate unexpected contact from weak ties more than you think) justify the activity itself and argue against the self-censorship that stops small organisations from asking; the 34x face-to-face vs email gap argues for pushing cold contact toward richer channels (phone, in-person, video) whenever feasible, especially for a local organisation.
- The compliance techniques map onto message elements: "because" = state a concrete, specific reason for contacting this person now; pique = odd/specific numbers and unexpected openers; BYAF = explicit easy out ("no problem if not"); FITD = ask for a micro-commitment (opinion, 15-second reply) before a meeting; advice-seeking = frame the CTA as asking for guidance; local social proof = name peer organisations in the same city/sector; pratfall = candid admission of smallness only after competence is established.
- Replication record (BYAF low-risk g = 0.11; FITD r ≈ 0.17; "even a penny" meta null on totals; first-name personalization null in 2023) means none of these is a reliable lever on its own; treat them as hygiene, not strategy.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve original-paper percentages for Langer's large-request condition, Goldstein's towel conditions, Burger's birthday study, or Cialdini & Schroeder; numbers above come from secondary summaries where stated.
- Did not search Cialdini 1975 door-in-the-face, Epley & Schroeder 2014 directly, or loss-aversion framing experiments in outreach.
- No peer-reviewed experiment on "specificity" of numbers in cold B2B emails was found (precision-signals-confidence literature exists in negotiation but was not retrieved).

## KQ2. What A/B tests / large-sample analyses of cold email and cold call elements have been published (subject lines, question vs statement, length, CTA type, P.S., video, plain text)?

### Takeaway
The best-documented element-level results are vendor analyses, not controlled A/B tests: Gong (304,174 emails) found an "interest CTA" beats a meeting-time CTA in first-touch cold email, while a specific-time CTA wins later in a deal (37% vs 25%); ROI language cuts cold-email success 15%; plain-text beats HTML-heavy email (HubSpot); 1-3 questions and ~3rd-grade reading level correlate with more replies (Boomerang, 40M emails); and modest, contextual personalization correlates with ~50% higher reply rates (Hunter, 11-34M emails). Evidence for question-vs-statement subject lines, P.S. lines and video thumbnails is weak, inconsistent or purely promotional.

### Cited Findings

**CTA type (interest vs meeting)**
- [VENDOR] Gong Labs, 304,174 emails, success = meeting booked within 10 days: three CTA types compared (Specific day/time; Open-ended meeting; Interest = "would it be worth exploring?"). Interest CTA was the top performer for cold emails (exact % only shown in chart; not stated in text). In deal-stage emails the ranking flips: Specific 37%, Open-ended 32%, Interest 25%. Specific CTA success rises from 15% (cold) to 37% (deal stage) — [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings)
- [VENDOR] Hunter 2025 (11M emails, AI-classified follow-ups): the most effective follow-up type is the referral request ("who is the right person?") — [Hunter State of Cold Email 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- [PRACTITIONER] Predictable Revenue (Aaron Ross, Salesforce): short plain-text email to an executive asking only for a referral to the right person; summaries report 7-10% reply rates vs <1% for cold dials at the time — [Anymail Finder summary](https://blog.anymailfinder.com/predictable-revenue-46db52f32a69); [Pulse RevOps notes](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/bs0236)
- [PRACTITIONER] Chris Voss's "no-oriented" revival email "Have you given up on this project?" is recommended for stalled threads; Big Think reports replies often within minutes, but no controlled data; a "23% success rate" seen on a training site is unsourced — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/videos/chris-voss-on-walking-away-from-a-negotiation/)

**Length, reading level, questions**
- [VENDOR] Boomerang 2016, ~40M emails (general business email, not only cold): 50-125 words had the highest response (~50%); 25-word and 2,000-word emails both ~44%; >2,500 words <35%. Reading level: 3rd-grade 53% vs kindergarten 46% vs college 39% (a 36% relative lift for 3rd grade over college). Emails with 1-3 questions were 50% more likely to get a reply than those with none; 3 questions beat 8+ by 20% — [Boomerang blog](https://blog.boomerangapp.com/2016/02/7-tips-for-getting-more-responses-to-your-emails-with-data/); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2016/03/02/ignored-emails-communication-work)
- [VENDOR] Hunter, 34M cold emails: 20-39 words had the highest average reply rate (4.5%); overall 3.7-4.5% depending on length; author warns matching the range will not by itself raise replies — [Hunter word count](https://hunter.io/blog/cold-email-word-count)
- [VENDOR] Hunter 2025 report, 11M emails: correlation between word count and reply rate only -0.09; suggests plain text, 40-60 words; images showed no statistically significant difference in reply rate — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- [VENDOR] Gong: longer cold emails were "significantly more effective" at booking meetings (chart only, no numbers) — conflicts with Hunter/Lavender short-email guidance — [Gong email stats](https://www.gong.io/blog/sales-email-statistics)
- [VENDOR] Lavender benchmark, 231,818 recent cold emails from ~50k inboxes (as of Feb 2026): emails scoring 90+ on Lavender's rubric (short, low reading level, few questions, mobile-friendly, "you"-focused) show a reply-rate lift vs average; e.g., Operations persona 5.4% reply (58% lift) for A-grade emails; Finance: only 6.1% of emails earn an A and A-grade lift is 79%. Lavender does not publish reply rate by word count/reading level separately — [Lavender benchmark report](https://www.lavender.ai/blog/the-cold-email-benchmark-report). Lavender's marketed "20.5% average reply rate" is self-reported and Hunter notes it appears to include replies beyond first response — [Hunter statistics](https://hunter.io/blog/cold-email-statistics)

**Language that hurts**
- [VENDOR] Gong, 132,000+ emails: ROI terms, multipliers or percentages in a cold email reduce success by 15% — [Gong email stats](https://www.gong.io/blog/sales-email-statistics)
- [VENDOR] Gong cold calls: opener "Did I catch you at a bad time?" = 40% less likely to book; "How've you been?" = 6.6x; "reason for my call" = 2.1x; successful calls are ~2x longer, rep talks ~55%, opening monologue 53s vs 25s in unsuccessful calls; successful calls use 65% more "we" (collaborative language) — [Gong cold-call stats](https://www.gong.io/blog/cold-call-stats)

**Personalization**
- [VENDOR] Hunter 2025 (11M emails): small campaigns (≤50 recipients) 5.8% reply vs 2.1% for 1,000+ recipients; custom attributes + manual edits associated with the highest replies (no effect size given). Secondary summary of Hunter's 31M-email data: 2 custom attributes in body 5.6% vs 3.6% with none (correlational) — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025); [Rework summary](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/outbound-prospecting/personalization-at-scale)
- [VENDOR] Belkins, 5.5M 2024 emails (via secondary): personalized subject lines 46% open vs 35% — [Whali summary](https://whali.co.uk/blog/cold-email-subject-lines-analysis)
- [VENDOR] Third-party analysis of Lavender's sample: personalized emails beat templates by 50-250% depending on department (secondary; treat cautiously) — [Something Inc.](https://somethinginc.com/blog/cold-email-sounds-like-ai-lavender-data/)

**Subject lines: question vs statement**
- [VENDOR] Yesware, 1.2M cold subject lines: question subject lines ~10% above average open rate — [Yesware](https://www.yesware.com/blog/cold-email-subject-lines/)
- [VENDOR] Autobound (130M+ emails, via Webtonic): 21% open lift for questions over statements; methodology not shown — [Webtonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/email-subject-lines)
- [VENDOR, nonprofit A/B] NextAfter/Care Net 2018 test: statement line slightly beat question (15.8% vs 14.7%), newsletter not cold outreach — [NextAfter](https://www.nextafter.com/?p=15034)
- [VENDOR] Hunter: 2025 framing is that opens are the wrong goal; replies are; day/season of send showed no meaningful effect — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)

**Plain text vs HTML**
- [VENDOR A/B] HubSpot internal tests (~2015): HTML template with images had 21% lower click-through than plain text and, combined with lower opens, 51% fewer clicks; GIF test: plain text 42% more clicks; opens dropped 23-37% as HTML richness increased; single-newsletter case: 15.3% open/4.1% CTR plain vs 13.2%/1.8% HTML — [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/plain-text-vs-html-emails-data)
- [VENDOR] Hunter 2025: tracking pixels associated with lower reply rate (no number); webmail senders (gmail.com) underperform custom domains; Outlook + custom domain had the highest average reply (5.9%) — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)

**Images / video thumbnails / P.S.**
- [VENDOR] lemlist: campaigns with images "can have up to 42% higher CTR"; single showcased campaign 92% open / 32% reply; agency claim of 17% avg reply on visually personalised sequences vs 3-5% flat text is not in lemlist's own published data — [lemlist images post](https://proxy.lemlist.com/blog/images-in-cold-emails); [Hack'celeration agency page](https://hackceleration.com/agency/lemlist)
- [VENDOR] Contradicting: Hunter 2025 found no statistically significant reply difference for emails with images, and images can hurt deliverability on low-reputation domains — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- No published A/B data on P.S. lines was found (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- The one robust cross-source pattern is "ask small, ask specific, write plainly": interest-based CTA, 1-3 questions, 20-125 words, no ROI claims, plain text, concrete contextual personalization. These align with Langer ("because"), FITD (micro-commitment) and Brooks (advice-seeking) from KQ1.
- Gong's "longer cold emails win" vs Hunter/Lavender "shorter wins" is likely a selection artefact: Gong's users sell high-ACV software to engaged accounts; Hunter's are high-volume senders. For a small organisation sending few, researched emails, moderately longer but highly specific is defensible.
- Element-level A/B evidence is thin enough that the correct operating conclusion is to run one's own tests (see KQ4 metrics), not to copy vendor "best subject lines".

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment found on P.S. lines, video thumbnails, or question-vs-statement subject lines in B2B cold email; all claims are vendor marketing or small nonprofit tests.
- Gong does not publish the actual percentage for the interest CTA; only the ranking.
- Reply.io and Lemlist first-party A/B data could not be retrieved in this pass.

## KQ3. What are the documented top failure causes (self-centred copy, generic templates, asking too much too early, wrong persona, no trigger, deliverability, giving up early, no CRM discipline), with numbers?

### Takeaway
Recipient surveys and large email datasets converge on three root causes: irrelevance (71% of decision-makers cite it), impersonality (43%) and lack of trust (36%); operationally the biggest leaks are deliverability (roughly 6-17% of cold emails never reach the inbox depending on dataset), over-long or over-frequent sequences that triple spam complaints, and under-following-up. The widely quoted "44% of reps give up after one follow-up" has no traceable primary source.

### Cited Findings

**Irrelevance / self-centred / generic**
- [SURVEY+VENDOR] Hunter 2025, 217 decision-makers + 11M emails: reasons for not responding: lack of relevance 71%, impersonality 43%, lack of trust 36%; 20% say they never receive a relevant cold email; only 24% get a valuable one weekly; average reply 4.1% (95.9% unanswered); 61% nonetheless say they WANT cold emails (71% in US), 29% prefer LinkedIn, 10% cold call; 67% don't mind AI use — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- [SURVEY] RAIN Group 2018, 488 buyers + 489 sellers: 82% of buyers accept meetings with sellers who proactively reach out (at least sometimes); 58% of most-recent meetings were initiated by email, 54% by phone; 52% of sellers say it takes 5-10 touches to connect for the first time — [RAIN press release](https://www.marketingdive.com/press-release/20180131-new-rain-group-center-for-sales-research-study-debunks-common-myths-about-s/); [RAIN PDF](https://www.rainsalestraining.com/hubfs/PDFs/Top%20Performance%20in%20Sales%20Prospecting.pdf)
- [VENDOR] Gong: ROI/percentage claims (talking about your value instead of their problem) cut cold-email success 15% — [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/sales-email-statistics)
- [VENDOR] Hunter: campaigns ≤50 recipients get 5.8% replies vs 2.1% at 1,000+, i.e., scale/templating correlates with a ~64% drop — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)

**Asking for too much, too early**
- [VENDOR] Gong: specific-time meeting CTA succeeds 15% in cold stage vs 37% once interest exists; interest CTA best for first touch — [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/this-surprising-cold-email-cta-will-help-you-book-a-lot-more-meetings)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Langer: placebic reasons only work for small requests; large requests need a real reason (KQ1).

**Wrong channel assumption**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Email requests to strangers ~34x less effective than face-to-face with identical scripts, and senders overestimate email by ~50 points (KQ1, Roghanizad & Bohns).
- [VENDOR] Belkins 2026 multichannel dataset (7.5M emails, 112k LinkedIn sequences, 46k calls): connect rate per prospect reached 18.6% calls, 5.04% LinkedIn, 0.45% email; LinkedIn messenger campaigns 12.22% reply vs email step-1 reply 0.59%; calls produced 33.6% of appointments and email 50% — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics?frame=0)

**Giving up too early vs. over-following-up (both documented)**
- [UNSOURCED] "44% of salespeople give up after one follow-up" and "80% of sales need five follow-ups" circulate across dozens of blogs with no primary study; wording varies (call vs email vs after one "no") — [Cirrus Insight stats page noting lack of origin](https://www.cirrusinsight.com/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics); [SalesLion](https://saleslion.io/sales-statistics/44-of-salespeople-give-up-after-one-follow-up-call/)
- [VENDOR] Woodpecker: 9% avg reply with no follow-up vs ~13% with ≥1; experienced users 16% vs 27%; first follow-up gets ~40% higher reply than the initial email; recommends 2-3 follow-ups, ≥2-3 days apart; 5th-7th add "a fraction" — [Woodpecker](https://woodpecker.co/blog/follow-up-statistics/)
- [VENDOR] Hunter 2025: 4.9% reply with follow-ups vs 3.0% without; 48% of recipients appreciate follow-ups, 27% don't mind, 25% find them annoying; unsubscribes rise after the 3rd follow-up — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- [VENDOR] Belkins 2025 study (16.5M emails, 93 industries, Jan-Dec 2024): highest reply rate (8.4%) from the first email, declining with each follow-up; 4+ emails in a sequence more than triples unsubscribe and spam-complaint rates; smaller companies tolerate more follow-ups, enterprise goes cold fast; full-campaign average 5.8% in 2024, down from 6.8% in 2023 — [Belkins study](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics?frame=0); [secondary summary](https://prospeo.io/s/cold-email-case-study)
- [VENDOR] Belkins 2026 update (7.5M emails, 2025): step 1 = 41.4% of replies, steps 2-6 = 58.6%; steps 3-5 generate 53.5% of email-sourced meetings; appointment rate ~0.2% from step 3 onward (about double steps 1-2). Note the article's figures are internally inconsistent on the share of meetings from steps 1-2 — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics?frame=0)
- [VENDOR] lemlist/Instantly (secondary): campaigns with 4-7 emails ~27% reply vs ~9% for 1-3; attribution unclear — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/follow-up-emails-vs-no-followup/)
- [VENDOR] InsideSales.com (1,456+ cadences, 2019): contact rate drops sharply after 7 attempts; 6-8 day cadences with 1-2 day spacing had highest contact rates; responding within 24h to a link click = 3.7x contact rate — [MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2019/40610/the-optimal-sales-cadence-frequency-and-duration-benchmarks)

**Deliverability**
- [POLICY] Google/Yahoo bulk-sender rules effective 1 Feb 2024 (≥5,000 msgs/day): SPF + DKIM + DMARC (p=none minimum, alignment required), one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) honoured within 2 days, spam complaint rate below 0.3% (target <0.1%) — [Bounteous](https://www.bounteous.com/insights/2024/01/31/2024-gmail-and-yahoo-deliverability-changes/); [Close help centre](https://help.close.com/docs/requirements-for-bulk-email-senders)
- [VENDOR] Inbox placement: Validity 2025 global benchmark 83.5% inbox / 6.7% spam / 9.8% missing (marketing email); Smartlead Q1-Q3 2025 (3.2M+ cold emails from its customers) Q3 inbox 72.87%, spam 8.64% (Q1 spam 15.47%); SalesHandy 2026 (53M cold emails) 95.2% inbox — [Smartlead PDF](https://marketing-assets.smartlead.ai/guides/Cold_Email_Blueprint_Q1_Q3_2025.pdf); [Warmforge roundup](https://www.warmforge.ai/blog/email-deliverability-statistics); [SalesHandy](https://saleshandy.com/blog/email-deliverability-statistics)
- [VENDOR] Hunter 2025: webmail sender addresses and tracking pixels both associated with lower reply rates — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- [VENDOR] Instantly guidance: cap ~30 sends per inbox per day with gradual warm-up — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)

**Wrong persona / list quality**
- [PRACTITIONER] Predictable Revenue: the bottleneck is identifying the right person, which is why the referral ask to a senior contact works — [Anymail Finder](https://blog.anymailfinder.com/predictable-revenue-46db52f32a69)
- [VENDOR/SURVEY] Bridge Group 2025 (351 companies): only 60% of SDRs at quota, lowest in the study's history; median Stage-0 meeting quota fell 40% since 2018 (to 10/month), consistent with rising difficulty — [Bridge Group 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)

**CRM discipline**
- No quantitative study tying CRM hygiene specifically to cold-outreach reply rates was found; Bridge Group reports 82% of teams align SDRs to AE territories and that the median SDR logs 112 activities/day, implying tracking is a precondition for the cadence math below — [Bridge Group 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)

### Inferences
- The follow-up data are not contradictory once denominators are separated: per-email reply rate falls with each step (Belkins), but cumulative replies and especially meetings keep accruing through step 3-5, with spam/unsubscribe cost climbing steeply after email 3-4. The evidence-based rule is therefore 3-4 emails (1 + 2-3 follow-ups) spaced ≥2-3 days, embedded in a multichannel cadence rather than an 8-email blast.
- "Lack of trust" (36%) is the failure mode most under-addressed by copy tips and most addressable by warm intros, local proof and richer channels (KQ5).
- Failure to follow up and over-following-up are both real; the "44%" stat should not be cited as fact.

### Gaps
- No primary source for the "44% give up after one follow-up" or "80% need 5 follow-ups" claims.
- No controlled data on "wrong persona" rates or CRM-discipline effects; only practitioner assertions.
- Lavender's "I/we vs you" language data (often quoted in talks) could not be located in a published form.

## KQ4. How do high-performing teams structure the operation: ICP and tiering, trigger events, list-building tools, writing/QA tools, sequencing tools, dashboards, cadence (touches over days) and team structure (SDR/BDR)?

### Takeaway
The reference operating model (Bridge Group 2025, n = 351 B2B companies) is a specialised SDR function at ~1 SDR per 2.4 AEs, ~6.4 SDRs per manager, median 112 activities/day (44 calls, 41 emails, 19 LinkedIn) yielding ~4 quality conversations and a median quota of 10 held meetings/month; cadences across Salesloft, Yesware, InsideSales and Belkins converge on 8-12 multichannel touches over 2-4 weeks, front-loaded in the first 10 business days, with ≥1-day and ≤5-day gaps; the stack is four layers (list/ICP, enrichment, multichannel execution, CRM/reporting).

### Cited Findings

**Team structure and benchmarks (SDR/BDR model)**
- [SURVEY] Bridge Group 2025 SDR Metrics (10th ed.; 351 B2B companies, 78% North America, 83% SaaS, $47M median revenue, $50K median ASP; observational, skews to engaged SD leadership): SDR:AE ratio 1:2.4 (most common 1:2, 31%); 6.4 SDRs per first-line leader (3.6 at <$5M revenue); 60% report to Sales, 26% to Marketing; 82% align SDRs to AE territories; median monthly quota 10 Stage-0 meetings (intro model 16, semi-qualified 10.4, fully-qualified 9) and 6 Stage-1 converted opps; 60% of SDRs at quota; tenure 1.9 yrs; ramp 3.0 months; annual attrition 40% median (13% involuntary, 11% voluntary, 16% promotions); 4.1 quality conversations/day; median 112 activities/day (44 phone, 41 email, 19 LinkedIn, 8 other); phone-centric teams 56 dials and 4.6 QCs/day vs email-centric 28 dials and 3.4 QCs; median OTE $80K (55/25 split); pipeline per SDR $3.78M/yr — [Bridge Group 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group); [Bridge Group tenure/ramp](https://bridgegroupinc.com/research/sdr-tenure-and-ramp-time)
- [PRACTITIONER] Predictable Revenue (Ross & Tyler): specialise roles (inbound qualifiers vs outbound SDRs vs closers vs account managers); outbound via short referral emails to executives rather than cold dials — [Pulse RevOps summary](https://pulserevops.com/knowledge/bs0009)
- [PRACTITIONER] The Sales Development Playbook (Bertuzzi 2016): segment by account size/motion; enterprise SDRs run named-account plays with 50-200 target accounts each; account-based prospecting is not worth it below ~$50K/yr deal value — [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/books/blog/the-sales-development-playbook-by-trish-bertuzzi.html); [Revenue.io podcast w/ Bertuzzi](https://www.revenue.io/podcasts/winning-with-account-based-strategies-w-trish-bertuzzi-episode-430)
- [PRACTITIONER] Fanatical Prospecting (Blount): 30-Day Rule (prospecting done in any 30-day window pays off over the next 90 days, so daily time-blocked prospecting is non-negotiable); Universal Law of Need (desperation lowers close rates, so keep the pipeline full); Law of Familiarity (reduce friction by raising recognisability before the ask) — [SuperSummary](https://www.supersummary.com/fanatical-prospecting/summary/); [Fin24 review](https://www.fin24.com/Opinion/Columnists/BOOK-REVIEW-Fanatical-prospecting-20151026)

**Cadence design (touches, days, channel mix)**
- [VENDOR] Salesloft eBook (averages of best-performing cadences): execute a high number of touches in the first 10 business days with gradually widening gaps, then nurture 1-2x/month; single-channel cadences get much lower response than multichannel; most top cadences start with a call before an email; A/B test your own — [Salesloft eBook PDF](https://pages.salesloft.com/rs/432-WAJ-793/images/33%20Tips%20for%20Optimizing%20B2B%20Sales%20Emails%20eBook.pdf)
- [VENDOR] Yesware, 33M email/phone activities from 8 tech companies >$100M revenue (LinkedIn not tracked): campaigns averaged 7.56 touches; recommends 2-4 week cadences with 1-5 day gaps; best-performing example: call D1, email D2, call D5, call D7, email D12, email D14 (6 touches/13 days, 3 calls + 3 emails); at touch 3 calls and emails reply 18% vs 16%; cites other studies finding 8-9 touches optimal — [Yesware cadence](https://www.yesware.com/blog/sales-cadence/)
- [VENDOR] InsideSales.com, 1,456+ cadences: contact rate falls sharply after 7 attempts; 6-8 day duration with 1-2 day spacing had highest contact rate; replying within 24h to engagement = 3.7x contact — [MarketingProfs](https://www.marketingprofs.com/charts/2019/40610/the-optimal-sales-cadence-frequency-and-duration-benchmarks)
- [VENDOR] Belkins 2026: steps 3-5 produce 53.5% of email-sourced meetings; calls 18.6% connect per prospect; LinkedIn 7%+ reply all year; recommends email + LinkedIn + phone — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics?frame=0)
- [VENDOR] Cognism 2024 cold-calling report: of 9,247 connected calls, 5,265 conversations and 254 meetings (4.82% conversation-to-meeting, up from ~2% in 2023); connect rate ~16.6%; 93% of conversations happen by the third call attempt; Tuesdays best for booking; 2025 report: 6.7% meeting rate (UK 8%, EU 6%, US 6%) for Cognism users — [Cognism statistics](https://cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-statistics); [2024 report PDF](https://www.cognism.com/hubfs/2024-Cold-Calling-Report-PDFs.pdf)
- [VENDOR] Instantly: 1-3 follow-ups over 7-14 days; first follow-up adds ~40-50% more replies; reply-rate tiers: broad ICP/new domain = low single digits; segmented ICP + verified data + 1-2 follow-ups = 5-10%; high-intent triggers + multi-point personalization = 10-20% (editorial estimates) — [Instantly benchmarks](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)
- [PRACTITIONER] Outreach's SDR org used only seven sequences, printed on a note card at every desk; Sam Nelson's "Agoge" team at Outreach popularised a hyper-personalised-first-touch, heavy-follow-up, multichannel sequence (exact step list not retrievable from primary sources) — [Outreach: how Outreach uses Outreach](https://www.outreach.ai/resources/blog/how-outreach-uses-outreach-to-sell-outreach); [Outreach webinar post](https://www.outreach.io/blog/streamline-and-shine-how-to-double-your-sales-email-response-rates); [Sybill description](https://sybill.ai/blogs/agoge-sequence-to-nurture-your-prospects)

**Trigger events / signals**
- [VENDOR/ANECDOTAL] Self-reported 6-week test: moving from scheduled batches to messages sent within 24-48h of an observable prospect signal raised replies from 3-4% to ~18%, copy unchanged — [Tyler Willis](https://tylerewillis.com/blog/trigger-based-outreach-reply-rate)
- [VENDOR] Glossary/vendor estimates: trigger-timed sequences 2-5x response vs calendar cadences; new executives "10x more likely" to bring in new vendors in first 90 days; 71% of newly funded companies finalise a vendor decision within 90 days; leadership-change outreach ~14% response — all unverified vendor figures — [Komo glossary](https://komo.ai/glossary/trigger-based-outreach); [Recruiterflow](https://recruiterflow.com/blog/trigger-based-recruitment-bd/)
- [VENDOR] Instantly ties the 10-20%+ reply tier to "high-intent triggers" — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)

**Tooling categories**
- [VENDOR] Four-layer stack: (1) list building/ICP targeting: LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo, Cognism; (2) enrichment/waterfall: Clay (does not send email itself), La Growth Machine; (3) multichannel execution/sequencing: Instantly, Smartlead, Outreach, Salesloft, Apollo sequences, lemlist, Reply.io; (4) CRM/reporting: HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive. - [VENDOR] Writing/QA: Lavender scores emails (length, reading level, questions, mobile preview, personalization) and reports lift for 90+ scores; benchmark built on ~50k inboxes — [Lavender](https://www.lavender.ai/blog/the-cold-email-benchmark-report)
- [VENDOR] Deliverability tooling: warm-up and inbox rotation (Instantly/Smartlead), Google Postmaster Tools for complaint rate, custom sending domains — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025); [Bounteous](https://www.bounteous.com/insights/2024/01/31/2024-gmail-and-yahoo-deliverability-changes/)

**Metrics and iteration**
- [VENDOR] Instantly defines: reply rate; positive reply rate = interested/qualified replies ÷ replies; effective reply rate = positive + qualified-neutral; meetings per 1,000 sends; time-to-first-meeting — [Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)
- [VENDOR] Benchmarks to calibrate against: average cold-email reply 4.1-4.5% (Hunter 11M/34M emails); Belkins full-campaign 5.8% (2024), first-email 8.4%; Backlinko/Pitchbox 8.5% is link-building/PR outreach, not sales — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025); [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics?frame=0); [SerpSculpt note on Backlinko](https://serpsculpt.com/average-cold-email-response-rate-b2b/)
- [VENDOR] Call funnel gates to track separately: dial-to-connect (~16-19%), connect-to-conversation, conversation-to-meeting (~5-7%), dial-to-meeting (~2-3%) — [Cognism](https://cognism.com/blog/cold-calling-statistics); [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics?frame=0)
- [SURVEY] Bridge Group: quality conversations per day (4.1) and held-meeting quota (10/mo) are the activity and output anchors used by SD leaders — [Bridge Group 2025](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)

### Inferences
- A defensible default cadence from the convergent data: ~8-10 touches over 14-21 days, e.g., Day 1 call + email, Day 2 LinkedIn connect, Day 4 call, Day 5 email #2, Day 8 call + voicemail, Day 10 email #3 (referral/"who is the right person" ask), Day 14 LinkedIn message, Day 18-21 break-up/"have you given up" email; then nurture monthly. Emails capped at 3-4 to stay under the spam-complaint knee (Belkins/Hunter), calls carrying the extra touches (Yesware/Salesloft).
- Research routine implied by the data: tier accounts (Bertuzzi: named accounts only above a value threshold), spend research time on Tier 1 only, and let triggers decide WHO gets contacted THIS week rather than alphabetical list order.
- Minimum viable dashboard: sends/dials per day; inbox placement + complaint rate (<0.1%); reply rate; positive reply rate; meetings booked per 100 contacts; show rate; by segment, by sequence, by first-touch variant. Iterate one variable at a time with ≥100-200 contacts per arm; element effects in the literature are 10-50% relative, so smaller samples cannot detect them.

### Gaps
- Bridge Group does not publish touches-per-prospect or accounts-per-SDR in the 2025 edition; the oft-cited "8.2 attempts / 9-12 sweet spot" could not be traced to a specific edition.
- No primary documentation of the exact Agoge sequence steps.
- Trigger-event effect sizes come only from vendor glossaries and one self-reported test; no controlled study found.

## KQ5. How should a small, non-SaaS organisation (school, nonprofit, local service business) adapt this when approaching companies for partnerships, sponsorships or B2B sales — give-first offers, local proof, warm-intro leverage?

### Takeaway
The evidence favours a low-volume, high-relevance, relationship-first version of the same system: lean on richer channels and warm introductions (34x face-to-face effect; 65% of B2B leaders rate warm referrals the most effective tactic; vendor data put warm-intro reply rates at 27-60% vs 1-5% cold), make the first ask tiny and advice- or referral-shaped, use same-city/same-sector social proof, and present sponsorship as a business exchange with explicit deliverables (visibility, access to families/employees, CSR narrative), since that is what corporate sponsors report valuing.

### Cited Findings

**Channel and warm-intro leverage**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Face-to-face request to strangers ~34x more effective than identical email request (KQ1) — [UWaterloo](https://uwaterloo.ca/management-science-engineering/news/face-face-request-34-times-more-successful-email)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Unexpected contact from a weak tie is appreciated more than the sender expects (Liu et al. 2022; 5,900+ participants) — [APA](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2022/07/friends-enjoy-being-reached)
- [SURVEY] Norwest Venture Partners/Marketbridge 2025, 177 B2B sales & marketing leaders: 65% rated warm referrals the most effective outreach tactic vs 44% for inbound follow-up (reported secondhand) — [Boomerang summary](https://getboomerang.ai/post/warmbound-converts-better-than-cold)
- [VENDOR, secondhand] Salesloft 2024 analysis of 10M emails as cited by Brixon: warm-intro emails 42% open vs 18% cold, 27% response vs 3-5% cold, ~5x conversion to appointments — [Brixon Group](https://brixongroup.com/en/?p=6644). Other vendor ranges: 40-60% response for warm intros vs 1-5% cold (Draftboard); 50-80% response / 25-50% meeting for warm investor intros vs 1-5% / <1% cold (Launchrock). Inconsistent and unaudited; direction is consistent — [Draftboard](https://www.draftboard.com/blog/how-to-measure-the-impact-of-warm-intros-on-your-sales-pipeline); [Launchrock](https://www.launchrock.com/library/expert-advice/warm-intro)
- [SURVEY, secondhand] Commsor 2026: 82.4% of sellers say warm-intro deals close faster — [Boomerang](https://getboomerang.ai/post/warmbound-converts-better-than-cold)
- [VENDOR] Belkins: LinkedIn connection + message yields 8.18% reply vs 5.28% without a message; LinkedIn reply rates 7%+ vs email 0.59% in 2025 — [Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/sales-follow-up-statistics?frame=0)

**Give-first / reciprocity and advice-seeking asks**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Regan 1971: an unrequested small favour increased later compliance independent of liking (KQ1) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reciprocity_(social_psychology))
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Brooks et al. 2015: asking for advice raises perceived competence, especially on hard problems and when asked of the person directly (KQ1) — [MIT Sloan Review](https://sloanreview.mit.edu/?p=35844)
- [VENDOR] Hunter 2025: referral requests are the most effective follow-up type — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] FITD: small prior commitment raises later large-request compliance (76% vs 17%) — [SAGE](https://sk.sagepub.com/ency/edvol/embed/socialpsychology/chpt/footinthedoor-technique)

**Local proof**
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Goldstein et al. 2008: norms described at the most local level ("guests in this room") outperformed general norms and identity-based norms — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2008/08/080822160338.htm)
- [PEER-REVIEWED] Burger et al. 2004: incidental similarity (shared birthday/name) raises compliance when it comes from the requester and is not common — [Burger publications](https://www.scu.edu/cas/psychology/emeritus/jerry-burger/jerry-burger---list-of-publications/)

**What corporate sponsors/partners say they want (schools and nonprofits)**
- [SURVEY, secondhand] Sponsorship industry survey via Double the Donation: 81% of corporate respondents see moderate or significant business value in sponsorships; speaking opportunities, networking events and social media ranked most effective marketing deliverables — [Double the Donation statistics](https://doublethedonation.com/corporate-sponsorship-statistics/)
- [PRACTITIONER] Mission alignment with the company's stated giving pillars (check CSR reports) is the first filter; sponsors treat it as a business arrangement centred on visibility and want deliverables in a contract; employee-engagement/volunteering options matter — [Double the Donation best practices](https://doublethedonation.com/corporate-sponsorship-best-practices/); [NonprofitHub](https://nonprofithub.org/?p=31326); [Winmo](https://www.winmo.com/?p=14214)
- [PRACTITIONER, school-specific] School sponsorship pitch documents list what businesses get: naming rights, newsletter/app/entrance advertising, preferential event seating, access to families with young children ("a prime consumer market"), reputation as socially responsible, internship/recruiting pipeline; advice: be explicit about the exchange, ask the partner what they want (one partner asked for the school choir to perform at events), structure 2-3 tiered packages with fixed term, start with suppliers you already use, count in-kind goods/services, say thank you — [Cavendish Road SHS: Why businesses support local schools (PDF)](https://cavendishroadshs.eq.edu.au/SupportAndResources/FormsAndDocuments/Documents/Why%20Businesses%20Support%20Local%20Schools%20through%20Sponsorship.pdf); [Cavendish Road sponsorship packages (PDF)](https://cavendishroadshs.eq.edu.au/SupportAndResources/FormsAndDocuments/Documents/Sponsorship%20Packages.pdf); [Education World principal interview](https://www.educationworld.com/a_admin/takefive/takefive029.shtml); [Barn2 guide](https://barn2.com/?p=710354)

**Scale and operating adaptations**
- [VENDOR] Small campaigns outperform: ≤50 recipients 5.8% reply vs 2.1% at 1,000+ (Hunter) — [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)
- [VENDOR] Solo/small-team stack can run on ~€120/mo (Sales Navigator + free CRM + one sequencer) — [La Growth Machine](https://lagrowthmachine.com/tech-stack-outbound-sales-teams/)
- [POLICY] Below 5,000 messages/day the Google/Yahoo bulk-sender rules do not formally apply, but SPF/DKIM/DMARC on the organisation's own domain and a <0.1% complaint rate remain the practical standard; webmail (gmail.com) sender addresses correlate with lower replies — [Close help centre](https://help.close.com/docs/requirements-for-bulk-email-senders); [Hunter 2025](https://hunter.io/the-state-of-cold-email-2025)

### Inferences
- For a school or nonprofit, the "SDR system" compresses to: a tiered list of 50-200 local/sector-aligned companies (Bertuzzi's named-account range), a weekly research block that looks for triggers (new branch opening, hiring, CSR announcement, new local manager), a first touch that is warm-intro-first, phone/in-person-second, email-third, and a first ask shaped as advice or a referral ("who at [company] thinks about community partnerships?") rather than a sponsorship request.
- Give-first offers consistent with the evidence: something the company can use immediately and that is specific to them (a short recognition in the school newsletter, a student-made asset, an invitation to speak, early access to a family audience), delivered before the ask; the Regan result suggests the favour works even if the recipient did not request it, and Brooks suggests asking the company's advice on the partnership design raises your standing.
- Local proof should be the social-proof element: "three other businesses on [street/sector] already sponsor X" (Goldstein) beats national statistics; incidental similarity (alumni parents, shared neighbourhood) should be surfaced when genuine.
- Because reply rates for small, researched campaigns are higher (5.8%) and per-contact value is high, a small organisation should accept lower volume and higher per-contact effort, keep sequences to 3-4 emails plus calls/LinkedIn, and measure meetings per 100 contacts rather than reply rate alone.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed or audited dataset on cold outreach by schools/nonprofits to companies for sponsorship; all school-specific material is practitioner guidance and example packages.
- The primary sponsorship-decision-maker survey behind the "81%" figure could not be located.
- No controlled study on "give-first" offers in B2B partnership outreach; the inference rests on Regan 1971 and practitioner guides.
- Warm-intro conversion multiples are vendor-reported and inconsistent (5x to 30-50x); only the direction is well supported.
